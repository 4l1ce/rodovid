# Reading 18th–19th century Ukrainian church and census records

## Script and orthography
- Pre-1918 Russian: ѣ (=е), і (=и before vowels), ъ at word end, ѳ (=ф),
  ѵ (=и). Transcribe names in modern Russian spelling unless the person
  asks for exact letters; keep the original word in Примечания when the
  spelling itself matters (Третяк vs Третьяк).
- 18th-century hands: titlo abbreviations (сн҃ъ = сынъ, дщ҃и = дщерь,
  іѡаннъ), superscript letters (о҃, вдова → "вдва"), Cyrillic numerals
  (а=1, в=2, г=3, д=4, є=5, ѕ=6, з=7, и=8, ѳ=9, і=10, к=20, л=30, м=40,
  н=50, ѯ=60, ѻ=70, п=80, ч=90, р=100; ҂ before a letter = thousands).
- Months: "генварь" = январь; dates are Julian (Old Style). Do not
  convert unless asked; say "ст. ст." when giving them.

## Layout of a metric spread (1830s–1918 printed forms)
- Births: left page — counters мужеска / женска пола, month and day of
  birth and baptism, child's name; right page — estate and names of
  parents, faith, godparents, who baptized. Read both pages of one row.
- Marriages: number, date, groom (estate, name, faith, which marriage,
  age), bride (same), who married them, guarantors for each side.
- Deaths: counters, dates of death and burial, estate/name (children as
  "<father> сын/дочь <name>"), age, cause, who buried.
- Each month ends with "Итого в <месяце> родилось мужеска пола N, женска
  M" — use it to check you have every record.
- Refilmed spreads ("INTENTIONALLY REFILMED") and "SOILED DOCUMENT"
  labels are filming artefacts; note them, do not create records twice.

## Names and surnames
- Patronymic forms: "Антониев" = "Антонов" (Антон/Антоний); feminine
  "Антонова", "Данилова". Patronymic = father's given name — use it to
  connect generations.
- Feminine surnames: -ова/-ева/-ина ("Хоменкова", "Мисюрина" from
  Мисюра); widows -иха ("Грушукиха"); unmarried "девица".
- The same surname varies across books and priests: Грушук / Грушак /
  Грушач / Грушун; Бугай / Бугаенко; Шульга / Шулга / Шуга. Record what
  is written; link variants in the notes, not by silent normalisation.
- Church vs. vernacular names: Иоанн/Іван, Иаков/Яків, Феодор/Федір,
  Адария/Одарка/Дарія, Параскева/Параска.
- Misreading traps: А/Л/Д, И/Н/П, ш/щ/т in cursive, и/н; "Аврамиев" vs
  "Афанасьев" (a real case that rewrote a branch until re-read on a better
  scan). Re-read any patronymic that decides a link.

## Estates and ranks (Звание)
козак/козачка, малороссийский козак, мещанин, купец, крестьянин
(собственник, казенный, помещичий), солдат, запасный/отставной/билетный
рядовой, унтер-офицер, фельдфебель, дворянин, почетный гражданин,
"корнетский внук" (descendant of Cossack officers). Estate is part of
identity: two "Яков Антониев" with different estates may be different men.

## Cross-checks that raise confidence
- Surname of a bride confirmed by a guarantor with the same surname and
  patronymic (likely brother).
- Same godparents for several children → same family.
- Ages across records consistent within ±2 years (ages were often
  rounded).
- Monthly totals match the number of records read.
