# Reasoning patterns that held up in real cases

Each pattern says what it proves and its limit. Cite the pattern by name
in the ledger's reasoning.

1. **Patronymic chain.** A man's patronymic is his father's given name;
   follow it record by record (Петро Свиридович → Спиридон Афанасьевич →
   Афанасий Степанович). Limit: common names; confirm with estate, place
   and household. Re-read the patronymic on the best scan — one misreading
   ("Аврамиев" for "Афанасьев") can attach a branch to the wrong father.

2. **Godparents and witnesses as kin network.** The same godparent for
   several children → one family. An uncle or brother often stands as
   marriage guarantor (поручитель). Two marriages in different years with
   the same guarantor suggest the couples are related (Діомид Прокофьев →
   Тихон 1888 and Анна 1895). Limit: neighbours and priests' relatives
   also stand; needs ≥2 independent supports.

3. **Same mother, two fathers' names.** If two "fathers" with one surname
   have a wife with the same name and patronymic (Анна Корнилиева under
   Григорий and Георгий Афанасьевич Бугай), suspect one man written two
   ways or misread — check scans and ages before splitting.

4. **Elimination.** "He is the only man of that name and age in the
   parish register" → hypothesis, never confirmation. State the population
   searched (which register, how complete).

5. **Age arithmetic.** Ages in revisions/deaths give birth year ±2 (often
   rounded to 0/5). Compare across records; a 10-year mismatch means a
   different person or a misread digit.

6. **Household continuity across censuses.** Rumyantsev 1767 → confession
   registers 1787/1790/1811 → revisions 1850/1858: the same household
   members appear with ages advancing; widows' households (вдова X-иха,
   "в дворі Y") show where orphans and in-laws lived. Note "наслідний
   отцівський" (inherited from father) vs "по жені" (by marriage).

7. **Household subdivision.** Numbers like 823 / 823-в / 823-г (1920) or
   377/346 (two revisions) record the same family's branches.

8. **Estate trajectory.** козак → міщанин → почесний громадянин explains
   why a family moves between record series; search the next estate's
   records rather than declaring a gap.

9. **Parish jurisdiction.** Siblings are normally baptised in one parish;
   a "sibling" in a distant village parish is likely a different family.
   Hamlets belong to a parish church in another village.

10. **Absence is not evidence** unless the source is complete for that
    place and time (run coverage, check lost years and illegible pages).

11. **Family oral history** is a lead with weight: it is often right about
    occupations and places, often wrong about exact kinship degree. Look
    for the record that would show the stated link.

12. **Name sake.** Children were often named after a grandparent; useful
    as a weak support, never as proof.

13. **A corrected surname reopens every reading of it.** When a surname is
    shown to be misread (Грушач → Грукач: «к» read as «ш»), the same reader
    may have made the same mistake in every other source. List each record
    where that model read the surname (other years, the Rumyantsev
    Description, revisions) and re-read the surname blind there before any
    link built on the old spelling is kept. Links between the old and the
    new spelling (Грукач 1738 → «Грушак» 1766 → Грушуки) drop to
    «гіпотеза» until then. Limit: genuine spelling drift also happens;
    the re-reading decides, not the assumption of an error.

14. **Check the timeline before choosing what to read.** Before reading a
    year's registers for an event, place it between the dates already
    known (last record with the first wife, first record with the second;
    first child's birth). If the window falls outside the years to be read,
    the search cannot find it — say so instead of reading (a marriage dated
    1846–1849 cannot be in the 1839 and 1842 books).

15. **Namesakes in a printed rehabilitation or memorial list.** A person in
    «Реабілітовані історією» or a memorial book with the right surname,
    place and birth year is a lead, not a link: list every candidate
    father in the family tree with a matching given name and age, check the
    birth in the registers of that year, and remember that a married
    woman's surname is usually her husband's.
